For several millennia, sundials were the main technology available for keeping time in the daily life of different cultures. They stood in squares and on buildings, and monuments were often built specifically to mark the time. One example is the Tower of the Winds in Athens (Greece), which was built around 50 BC and still bears the hour markings of the eight vertical sundials spread across its octagonal structure.

At our society's current stage of technological development, sundials no longer serve a practical everyday purpose. Even so, they have become something much greater: works of art, educational tools, instruments for teaching and for sharing science and the history of how humanity has kept time across generations.

It was from this view of the world that we created the Accuratum project. We set out to develop a tool that lets anyone on Earth build a sundial, simply and accurately, for use in their own neighborhood.

Accuratum is Latin for accurate, made with care, meticulous. The Accuratum system consists of a website and a software library that make it easy to generate horizontal sundials for (almost) any place on the globe. We use the Python ecosystem, in particular the NumPy and Astropy libraries, to compute the shadow of the gnomon (the pointer) throughout the year, quickly and accurately, from data by the IERS (International Earth Rotation and Reference Systems Service) on the orientation of the Earth.

The project's repository is available as free software.
